Quentin Fruytier

I am a PhD Candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) at the University of Texas at Austin, where I am advised by Prof. Aryan Mokhtari and Prof. Sujay Sanghavi. My research focuses on Machine Learning and AI, specifically at the intersection of optimization theory, representation learning, and latent variable models.

My work aims to build more efficient and robust large-scale models. Most recently, our preprint PIG: Parallel Inconsistency Guidance (with Anton Xue, Sanjay Shakkottai and Sujay Sanghavi) trains a ~2M-parameter adapter that predicts when parallel decoding of a diffusion language model would diverge from sequential decoding, and uses it to decode faster at matched accuracy on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-3B (code, models). Earlier, Learning Mixtures of Experts with EM (ICML 2025) shows that EM can outperform Adam for training Mixture of Experts models, and my InterDigital internship on shaping the aggregate posterior of latent-variable models led to a patent and to Sculpting the Latent Space, accepted at SDM 2026.

I earned an MSc in Mathematics & Statistics from McGill university where I was advised by Prof. Tim Hoheisel and Prof. Abbas Khalili and a BSc in Joint Honours Mathematics & Computer Science, also from McGill University. This strong theoretical foundation, combined with hands-on experience from software engineering and data science internships, allows me to bridge the gap between AI theory and real-world application.

Research Publications
Diffusion Language Models
NEW   PIG: Parallel Inconsistency Guidance for Accelerating Diffusion Language Models
Quentin Fruytier, Anton Xue, Sanjay Shakkottai, and Sujay Sanghavi
arXiv preprint, 2026
Code   Models: LLaDA-8B-Instruct   Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-3B  

Diffusion language models decode many masked positions per forward pass, but tokens committed together cannot condition on one another, and accuracy drops as decoding gets more parallel. We frame the choice of what to commit around sequential–parallel inconsistency: whether the model would have produced the same tokens one at a time. A lightweight adapter (~2M parameters, under 0.1% of a forward pass) predicts this inconsistency from the current pass, and two decoders, PIG.INT and PIG.EXT, spend a regret budget on it. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-3B, across GSM8K, MATH-500, MBPP, HumanEval and MMLU, PIG reaches up to 1.7× more tokens per forward pass at matched accuracy than Fast-dLLM, and the adapter is trained once per backbone on a single data mixture.

Representation Learning
Sculpting the Latent Space: Disentanglement with WAE-MMD and Programmable Priors
Quentin Fruytier, Akshay Malhotra, Shahab Hamidi-Rad, Aditya Sant, Aryan Mokhtari, and Sujay Sanghavi
SIAM International Conference on Data Mining (SDM 2026)
Project Page   PDF   ArXiv  

We show that the standard KL-based regularizer in VAEs is an unreliable mechanism for learning disentangled representations. Our solution is the Programmable Prior Framework, a novel MMD-based method that explicitly engineers the latent space to achieve state-of-the-art disentanglement without the common reconstruction trade-off. To validate that our method learns mutually independent features, we also introduce the Latent Predictability Score (LPS), a new unsupervised metric for quantifying entanglement on large-scale datasets.

Optimization Theory
Learning Mixtures of Experts With EM: A Mirror Descent Perspective
Quentin Fruytier, Aryan Mokhtari, and Sujay Sanghavi
ICML 2025
OpenReview   PDF   ArXiv   Slides   Poster

We provide a theoretical analysis of the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm for training Mixture of Experts (MoE) models. Our work connects EM to Mirror Descent to establish new convergence guarantees and shows through theory and experiments that EM can be a faster and more accurate alternative to standard gradient descent.

A Review of the Expectation-Maximization Algorithm and its Applications to Mixture Models
Quentin Fruytier
MSc Thesis, 2023
OpenView

This thesis provides a comprehensive review of the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, a powerful tool for parameter estimation in models with latent variables. Spanning from its foundational concepts in the 1970s to its modern applications, this work covers the algorithm's convergence properties and practical uses in mixture models, serving as a valuable resource for researchers.

AI For Traffic Prediction
Quentin Fruytier

PDF   Project Page

This project analyzes Deep Learning methods for traffic prediction, a key challenge due to complex spatial and temporal patterns. We take a deep dive into the state-of-the-art DCRNN model, investigating how its feature space dimension impacts performance on the METR-LA dataset and proposing specific architectural improvements to enhance prediction accuracy.
