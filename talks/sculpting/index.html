The standard recipe

The variational autoencoder adds noise and a KL penalty

What we need: the aggregate q(z) = Eₓ[ q(z | x) ] to match the factorized N(0, I).
The VAE hopes to get there by pulling every q(z | x) toward N(0, I), one input at a time.

The other cost · reconstruction

VAE noise trades reconstruction for generation

Noise and the pull to N(0, I) buy generation and cost reconstruction: +2.2 dB (VAE), +5.1 dB (β-VAE) on average. Compression and editing need a deterministic autoencoder, with no widely accepted way to disentangle one.

Measuring dependence without labels · LPS

Can one latent be predicted from the others?

Why the score is safe to trust

LPS can only underestimate entanglement

R² of any regressor ≤ η², the share of a latent’s variance the others truly explain

No regressor beats the conditional mean, so an LPS of 0.5 proves at least half of the latent information is entangled. No labels needed.

The tool · maximum mean discrepancy

Comparing two distributions from samples alone

q(z) has no formula, only samples (a batch of codes). The prior we can sample too.

Programmable prior · compression

A quantizer only works if the codes have the shape it expects

So we set the prior to the quantizer’s distribution and train with the quantizer in the loop.

Left: 2-bit designs · right: a plain autoencoder on MNIST (d = 12) retrained for this talk, each latent over its 0.1–99.9% range

Compression results · CIFAR-10

Shape the codes to fit the quantizer

Train with the quantizer in the loop and a prior equal to the distribution the quantizer assumes, Gaussian or uniform.
